Why EnduranceHub

Three questions every athlete should be able to answer

Distance alone doesn't tell you whether you're building fitness or digging a hole. These do.

How hard was this week, really?

Every session's duration is multiplied by how hard it felt (RPE 1–10) and summed across the week — the session-RPE method sports scientists use to quantify training stress. Two easy hours and one brutal interval session can carry the same load, and only one of them shows up in your mileage.

  • Monday-anchored weeks that match your calendar
  • Distance bars and load line on a single chart
  • Instantly spot build weeks vs. recovery weeks

Am I actually getting faster?

Pace is tracked per session and averaged across your whole history, so improvement shows up as a trend instead of a feeling. Personal records update the moment a workout beats them — longest distance, longest duration and best pace, each with the date it happened.

  • Records recalculated on every save
  • Best pace ignores sub-1km efforts, so sprints can't fake a record
  • Average pace across all activities, not just runs

How it works

From empty dashboard to real insight in four steps

No devices to pair, no subscription, no onboarding survey. Log a session and the analytics appear.

Create your account

Register with your email and confirm the six-digit code we send. Your password is bcrypt-hashed before it ever touches the database.

Log a session

Type, distance, duration and how hard it felt. Add a note if you want to remember why the legs were heavy.

Load, pace, records and streaks recompute on every save — no refresh button, no waiting for a nightly job.

Use the weekly chart to plan the next block: push when load is flat, back off when it spikes three weeks running.

Reading your training load

Load is duration multiplied by perceived effort, summed each week — the session-RPE method, which works the same for a long run, a gym session or a football match. A steady rise of roughly ten percent a week is a sustainable build; three sharp weeks in a row is usually how injuries start. Flat load with improving pace means fitness is arriving, and that's the block worth repeating.
